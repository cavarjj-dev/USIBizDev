After you leave

The page in your hand is the work.

This is extra. Later. Optional. Do not copy anyone else’s sentence. Do not build a library. If the seven-day act is real and you want more, six things. Not a syllabus.

  1. David Brooks, “The Moral Bucket List.”

    The distinction you already heard, in his words. Then put it down. Do not turn it into a second plan.

    New York Times, 11 April 2015
  2. Edgar Schein, Humble Inquiry.

    Asking instead of telling. Use it with the person you named, not as a communications course. Any edition. Short.

    Publisher page
  3. Robert Kegan and Lisa Lahey, “The Real Reason People Won’t Change.”

    If the stop you circled keeps winning, this is a map. Do not redo the afternoon on paper.

    Harvard Business Review, November 2001
  4. Atul Gawande, “Personal Best.”

    A surgeon already at the top hired a coach. That is a Tuesday act, not a plaque. Observation from outside, on purpose.

    The New Yorker, 3 October 2011
  5. Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer, “The Power of Small Wins.”

    The seven-day act is supposed to be felt. Progress at this scale, not a monument.

    Harvard Business Review, May 2011
  6. Douglas Stone and Sheila Heen, Thanks for the Feedback.

    How to hear a challenge without putting the armor back on. Finishable. Not a script for the conversation you already named.
